# Kernel -20 plan, November 9, 2026

The owner asked for a maximal build: suspend, the RTC and UEFI writes
enabled, everything tested in as few boots as possible, with fixes taken
from other Glymur and Mahua machines. `-19` was built and staged but never
booted; `-20` replaces it, so its tests are folded in. Tests:
`docs/linux-runbook-20-2026-11-09.md` (two boots). The patch list and
sources are in `patches/README.md` ("Kernel `-20`").

## Suspend

On a Glymur laptop s2idle needs three things that Tyler Baker worked out
on the Zenbook A16:

1. **USB survives it.** The Glymur USB controllers lose power in s2idle; an
   xHCI with a device attached then resumes through a stale address (SMMU
   fault, `Host System Error`) and every device behind it is lost. Tyler's
   fix stops the controllers being wakeup sources and asks xHCI to reset on
   resume. Our dwc3 has no software property for that, so it is ported
   (`backports/0079`, Faisal Hassan's xhci-plat property, and `0084`).
2. **Wi-Fi and the Type-C stack survive it.** Abel Vesa's two laptops-tree
   HACKs for ath12k over MHI (`0077`: QRTR HELLO before MHI receive on
   resume; `0078`: thermal devices kept across the firmware recovery that
   resume can trigger) and his UCSI worker on the freezable workqueue
   (`0076`), so a plug event in suspend waits for the devices to resume.
3. **The SoC reaches its deep states.** Providers waiting for `sync_state`
   keep their boot votes, and the apps RSC then keeps DDR and the NoCs up
   (Tyler: "cxsd Count: 0, aosd Count: 0"). On this laptop the crypto
   engine binds from `-19` (`qce-core`); the GPU management unit still
   holds three clock controllers (`docs/gmu-sync-state-2026-11-06.md`). The
   fix is Akhil P Oommen's series, whose v2 has an open regression, so
   `-20` does not carry it; `check-suspend.sh --sync-state` releases those
   providers at runtime instead, as Tyler did, and logs the RPMh sleep
   counters (`qcom_stats`, `rpmh-stats` is in `glymur.dtsi`) before and
   after each cycle. Taniya Das's collapse voting for the PCIe GDSCs
   (`0081`, accepted) lets the PCIe domains (NVMe, Wi-Fi) collapse.

The lid (GPIO 92, `wakeup-source`, wake on opening), the power key
(PMK8850, test tree) and the keyboard are wake sources already. Suspend
stays masked until `check-suspend.sh` has run three cycles and the owner
keeps it; a one-shot unit masks it again at the next boot if a resume
hangs. Not taken: Tyler's gpio-keys "wake only on a real lid change"
(the A16's lid line pulses once in deep sleep on AC; whether ours does is
what the first cycles show).

## The RTC

`-19` already builds the ADSP's PMIC GLink RTC driver (Qualcomm's
`rtc-qcom-glink`, the service Windows reads as `\_SB.PRTC`). Konrad Dybcio
confirmed on 2026-09-11 that Glymur machines have no RTC UEFI variable
("unlike older Snapdragon laptops"), matching our `-17` result, so the
PMK8850 RTC with `qcom,uefi-rtc-info` stays off. The driver gives the time
and wake alarms (the suspend test uses one). It sets the time only with
`allow-set-time` on the `pmic-glink` node, which `-20` still leaves out:
this laptop's Windows keeps the RTC in local time (India Standard Time,
`RealTimeIsUniversal` unset), and the driver sends a time-zone field of
0 with every write. A Linux write would then move Windows' clock until
it next synchronises. `check-rtc.sh` reads whether the RTC holds UTC or
local time, logs the raw words the service returns (the time-zone field
Windows writes) and sets Linux to `set-local-rtc` if needed. Whether to
add `allow-set-time` is decided from that log. The posted v2 of the driver
(Kamal Wadhwa and Raj Aryan, 2026-11-03, "Fix broken RTC alarm support on
Qualcomm Glymur and Hamoa/X1E80100 boards") is not taken yet, although it
fixes a use-after-free on unbind and a timeout path where a late firmware
reply can be taken for the next request's: v2 drops the `allow-set-time`
gate and always writes the RTC on `set_time`, and with
`CONFIG_RTC_SYSTOHC` the kernel rewrites it every 11 minutes once NTP has
synchronised, which would move Windows' local-time clock. It goes in once
`check-rtc.sh` has shown what the RTC holds and Linux is set to match. The
v2 cover letter also confirms why the PMK8850 RTC cannot give alarms on
these chips: its alarm interrupt is routed to the firmware that implements
Windows' time and alarm device, which relays it to Linux only through
PMIC GLink.

## UEFI variable writes

Allowed by the owner on 2026-11-09. The kernel always could write
(the QSEECOM allowlist, `upstream/qcom-next/0009`); `-17` to `-19` kept
`efivarfs` read-only through `/etc/fstab`. `install-from-usb.sh` now
mounts it read-write (keeping a copy of the old `fstab`).
`check-efivars-write.sh` writes one 32-byte variable under this project's
own GUID, and after the reboot between the two test boots checks that it
survived and deletes it. Still off on purpose: the EFI pstore backend
(`CONFIG_EFI_VARS_PSTORE_DEFAULT_DISABLE=y`; crash logs written into the
store on every panic can fill it), and `grub-install` and fwupd stay
disabled (the boot order and firmware updates are not part of this
change). Booting without the USB (#27) can use writes later, but the SSD's
EFI partition is shared with Windows and stays untouched until the owner
decides.

## USB full and low speed (#6)

`0080`: the M31 eUSB2 PHY's SE0 timeout (Elson Serrao and Peter Chen,
Qualcomm, v2, reviewed by Konrad Dybcio, Abel Vesa and Krishna Kurapati,
tested on the Zenbook A16). Spurious pulses during speed negotiation can
leave the PHY's state machine stuck; with the timeout it resets after
2.5 ms of SE0. That is the step where our full- and low-speed devices fail
(`device descriptor read/64, error -71`), on every port, including USB-A,
which has no repeater. Together with `-19`'s LDO7_B rail for the USB-C
repeaters, the runbook's mouse test now covers both causes. Already in the
base: Krishna Kurapati's repeater power-leak fix.

`0083`: Konrad Dybcio's Glymur USB 3 PHY settings from the latest hardware
reference guide (reviewed by Abel Vesa). It touches the PHY that runs USB-C
SuperSpeed, which works, so the runbook checks a SuperSpeed stick on both
USB-C ports.

## Audio

`0085`: Srinivas Kandagatla's SoundWire page cache (accepted, in 7.4-rc2):
the controller rewrote the SCP page registers before every paged transfer,
which breaks the WCD9378's two-byte volume writes (the high byte is
dropped). Backported with a bound check, since our transfer function also
sees the broadcast address. The headset jack is new in `-19`, so this
matters from its first test.

## External displays and video (the -test-av device tree)

One boot with a device tree that turns on Iris (the Mahua video trials of
`-19`, unchanged) and DisplayPort over both USB-C ports. On `-15` a
monitor made the EDID read fail and the display controller then timed out
until the machine reset. Since `-17` the driver no longer reports a failed
link training as success (`backports/0031`, Oleg Keri, accepted). The DP
endpoints use two lanes and the combo PHYs lose `mode-switch`, so a monitor
never takes the USB 3 lanes: Qualcomm's change for Glymur laptops (Saurabh
Anand, in the laptops tree). Qualcomm's Glymur DP PHY series (Ritesh Kumar
and Nabige Aala, v6 of 2026-10-28, 11 patches, not accepted) is mostly in
the base already: patches 1–3 as FROMLIST commits (`c202053823b5`,
`45b4851e05b7`, `e154e22d9a0a`, among them the DP COM v8 register layout
fix for the PLL-ready polling) and an earlier revision of the init and
configure rework as "Rework DP PHY runtime configuration"
(`5323704e28fd`). Of the rest, 5, 6 and 11 apply on top, 4 needs a merge
against the base's `dp_aux_cfg2`, and 7–10 overlap the rework and apply
neither forward nor reverse. Mixing two revisions in the PHY that also
carries USB-C SuperSpeed is not done blind, so `-20` runs the base's DP PHY
code; if the monitor still fails, the boot B log decides which v6 changes
to port. The display test comes last in its boot, since a hang ends the
boot.

## Boot options

The second boot also drops Ubuntu's generic `clk_ignore_unused
pd_ignore_unused arm64.nopauth` (part D of the `-19` runbook): without
`pd_ignore_unused`, firmware-enabled power domains with no consumer can turn
off, which deep suspend needs.

## TPM and NPU

- **TPM** (#19): not enabled, on purpose. Qualcomm's posted driver needs
  QTEE service 81, which HP's TrustZone does not offer, and the TPM app
  keeps its state through Windows-side listeners (RPMB or files under
  `C:\Windows\System32\DriverData` on the BitLocker volume). A Linux TPM
  driver could leave that state unsaved or divergent, which would put
  BitLocker at risk (`docs/tpm-2026-11-06.md`). The owner approved working
  on it on 2026-11-09: the TPM is Qualcomm's TrustZone fTPM ("xCG fTPM"
  4.0.0.1), not Pluton; its TrEE service declares no RPMB dependency,
  while the UEFI-variable service does, so `-20`'s UEFI write test also
  bears on it; and `scripts/windows/tpm-storage-evidence.ps1` (elevated,
  read-only) collects the rest (`docs/tpm-2026-11-09.md`).
- **NPU** (#20): the kernel side is in every device-tree kernel already:
  the CDSP runs HP's signed firmware and `fastrpc` binds its compute banks
  (`-18` log). Missing was userspace. HP's Windows NPU driver
  (`qcnspmcdm8480`) carries the CDSP-side FastRPC shell signed for this
  laptop (`fastrpc_shell_3`), and the kit now installs it with
  `check-npu.sh`, which runs Qualcomm's `fastrpc_test` on the CDSP. Models
  need Qualcomm's AI Runtime (QNN) 2.39 for Linux, matching HP's
  `libQnnHtpV81Skel.so` (version `v2.39.0`); its download needs a Qualcomm
  account.

## Which kernel tree (asked on 2026-11-09)

Two trees carry Glymur laptops now, and Qualcomm's image CI
(`qcom-deb-images`) builds and tests both on the Glymur CRD:

- **qcom-next** (`qualcomm-linux/kernel`), our base: Qualcomm's integration
  tree, 7.3. Our commit `17bbb4b37bbc` is also tagged `qcom-7.3-20261102`,
  the product kernel Qualcomm's CI installs as `linux-image-qcom-7.3`. It
  has the Glymur SoC work as FROMLIST/PENDING commits, including what only
  it has: the PMIC GLink RTC driver.
- **qcom-laptops** (`linux-msm/laptops-kernel`), community-maintained:
  mainline 7.4-rc1 plus 295 commits (about 257 posted patches below a
  "from list" marker, about 37 local, HACK and WIP commits above it),
  rebased on 2026-11-08. Qualcomm's CI removed the separate
  `glymur-laptops` build on the grounds that qcom-laptops "is expected to be
  the only branch carrying laptop support in future". It already carries
  what `-20` ports by hand (Tyler Baker's and Abel Vesa's suspend fixes, the
  SoundWire and WCD9378 work), plus the full DP PHY v6, the GMU driver,
  memory-latency DVFS and Qualcomm's TPM driver; it lacks the RTC driver
  and the M31 SE0 fix.

The owner's decision (2026-11-09): stay on qcom-next for now and move the
base when Linux 7.4 is out; which tree to move to is decided then.
Until then the rule of 2026-10-17 holds: first-party base, ports from the
laptops tree one at a time. `-20` is built and
checked on it, and the product kernel line is the same commit. Revisit when
qcom-next moves to 7.4: that rebase absorbs most backports anyway, and if
laptop work by then lands only in qcom-laptops, a switch costs less than
the porting.

- Memory-latency DVFS (LLCC and DDR scaling over SCMI, laptops tree) and

- The crypto algorithm driver (`CRYPTO_DEV_QCE` now depends on `BROKEN`
  upstream).
- HDMI and the cameras' CAMSS path: not on this laptop (its cameras are
  USB).
