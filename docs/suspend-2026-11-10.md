# Suspend: why s2idle is the right mode, and why it is not yet a real sleep, November 10, 2026

The owner asked whether `-21`'s s2idle-only setting is a workaround. The
mode is right; the sleep it reaches is not.

## The mode

Windows on this laptop offers only "Standby (S0 Low Power Idle)"
(`powercfg /a`: S1, S2 and S3 "disabled when S0 low power idle is
supported"). Snapdragon laptops have no S3: Modern Standby is S0 idle, and
its battery life comes from the SoC reaching its deepest platform state
(DRIPS) while the system idles. Linux's equivalent is s2idle. The "deep"
mode Linux also offered is PSCI `SYSTEM_SUSPEND`, which this firmware
refuses (`last_failed_errno -95` on `-20`), and one such attempt hung. So
`MemorySleepMode=s2idle` only stops Linux trying a path Windows does not
use either.

## The sleep `-20` reached

`check-suspend.sh` logs the AOP sleep counters (`qcom_stats`) before and
after each cycle (`captures/2026-11-10-k20/suspend-test-20261110T141129.txt`):

| Counter | Meaning | Before | After the rtc cycle | After the lid cycle |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `cxsd` | CX rail collapsed | 0 | 0 | 0 |
| `aosd` | AOSS sleep, crystal off | 0 | 0 | 0 |
| `ddr` | DDR low-power mode | 0 | 0 | 0 |

Only the CPUs slept. The system resumed, but this is the state another X1E
laptop measured at about 4 W, against about 0.25 W once the SoC reaches
DRIPS (`github.com/justririll/x1e-sleep`, Asus Vivobook S15; Windows
Modern Standby there: about 0.23 W).

## Known blockers on X1E, checked against `-21`

From the Vivobook S15 work, which went from ~4 W to ~0.25 W:

| Blocker on X1E | Here |
| --- | --- |
| GPU RSC left awake by an inverted check in `a6xx_rpmh_stop()` | fixed in the base (`d9108bfdb746`) |
| Bluetooth UART keeping clocks and CX votes (`hci_uart` holds it open) | fixed in the base (`pm_runtime_force_suspend` in the system-sleep callbacks) |
| LPASS tx/rx macros holding their `macro`/`dcodec` votes forever | fixed in the base (Qualcomm's "manage clocks in runtime PM"; all four macros drop their clocks in runtime suspend) |
| The system power domain's DRIPS idle state and PDC wake | in the base: `domain_ss3` (`0x0200c354`, the same state as X1E) on `power-domain-system`, and the Glymur PDC |
| The EC not told about Modern Standby | done in `-21`'s EC driver (`0x23` = 1/0; the Vivobook's kernel-debugger capture of Windows shows the same write) |
| The eDP panel rail `regulator-always-on` (~1.2 W) | not here: our `vreg_edp` is not always-on |
| syscon taking the TCSR clock provider's `bi_tcxo` as its register clock and keeping it prepared, which pins the crystal vote | **present**: Glymur's `tcsr` is `"qcom,glymur-tcsr", "syscon"` with `clocks = <&rpmhcc RPMH_CXO_CLK>`. Blocks `aosd` at least. Not upstream |
| pcie-qcom keeping its full-bandwidth vote, which pins DDR at 2092 MHz | untested here; debatable upstream |

Specific to this laptop:

- **Three clock controllers wait for `sync_state`** because of the GPU
  management unit (`gcc-glymur`, `gpucc-glymur`, `gxclkctl`, pending due to
  `3d6c000.gmu`), so they keep every boot vote, CX included. The fix is
  Akhil P Oommen's "drm/msm: Attach a driver to GMU" (v2 has an open
  regression, v3 not posted, `docs/gmu-sync-state-2026-11-06.md`).
  `check-suspend.sh --sync-state` releases them at run time; `-20` never got
  that far.
- **Power domains on before suspend:** the USB controllers' and PCIe 4/5's
  GDSCs, `cx` at 256, `mmcx`. Whether they stay on in suspend is not known.
- **On the deep attempts** all four USB controllers logged `HS-PHY not in
  L2` and PCIe 5 (the SSD) `Timeout waiting for L2 entry`. Whether the same
  happens in s2idle is not known.

## What `-21`'s boot measures

`check-suspend.sh` now prints, per cycle, the time spent in `cxsd`, `aosd`
and `ddr` (AOP timer ticks, 19.2 MHz) and the battery drain in mW; offers a
10-minute cycle on battery for a real drain figure; and lists before the
cycles what can keep the SoC up (crystal and RPMh clock users, interconnect
votes, regulators, the DSPs, PCIe link power management). The runbook runs
it plainly and then with `--sync-state`. The three outcomes decide `-22`:

- `cxsd` stays 0 even with `--sync-state`: something holds CX; the blocker
  list says what (USB, PCIe, a DSP, an interconnect vote).
- `cxsd` moves only with `--sync-state`: the GMU is the blocker, and `-22`
  needs the GMU series (or its v3) instead of a runtime release.
- `cxsd` moves but `aosd` stays 0: the crystal vote, very likely the TCSR
  syscon clock; `-22` can carry a fix for that.

One warning from the X1E work: with AOSS sleep reached, that laptop reset
about once an hour in long sleeps, and it now inhibits AOSS sleep (CX
collapse and DDR low power stay allowed: 0.17 W overnight). If this laptop
reaches `aosd`, long sleeps need watching.

Windows' own figure for this laptop needs `powercfg /sleepstudy` from an
elevated prompt (read-only): its DRIPS percentage and drain are the target.
